Computational Biology · Research Update

Conditioned generation of biosynthetic gene clusters with Evo2

A fine-tuned 7B genome language model encodes compound class internally but will not generate one on request. A staged programme has now closed every inference-time route — labels, guidance, steering, soft prefixes — and the closures are mechanistic rather than merely empirical: we can say what fails and why. One thing does work. Seed the model with a real fragment and it extends it in the right class, at four times the mismatched floor.

Model · Evo2 7B + LoRA Data · splits_core (47.5k / 22 classes) Updated · Aug 12, 2026 Status · reframed — detection is the bottleneck Works · exemplar conditioning 0.283

The one-paragraph version

The fine-tuned model writes realistic bacterial DNA and reads the class tag, but never builds the requested machinery. A controlled probe sweep ruled out capacity, class imbalance, chunk labeling and gene-aware chunking; a real-scale whole-core run came back negative and degraded with more training. Two experiments then reframed the problem: a linear probe recovers compound class from untouched base-Evo2 activations at 0.91 against 0.09 chance, and seeding with a real exemplar reaches 0.283 while the written label stays completely inert. The model knows the class and will not take a label.

That launched a steering programme to write the class in directly. It is now closed, and closed mechanistically. An early diagnostic caught a serious instrument bug — the "class directions" were really the sequence-length axis — and after rebuilding them properly, steering still failed at every layer, dose and recipe. A continuous readout pinned the shape of the failure: the effect on the target class is null at every dose — steering installs nothing. (A companion result, that the direction reliably deletes a class, was retracted the same day as a probe-leakage artefact.) The decisive test came last: the injected edit was 1.5–10× larger than needed to completely flip a linear class readout in the very layer it was applied to, at all nine depths tried — the edit lands, and the output does not follow. Ablation succeeds in activation space too, so the delete/install asymmetry is about how the model reads that space, not about where the classes sit in it. A companion audit found we had also been overdosing by 3–20 standard deviations, pushing activations clean off the data distribution, which finally explains why larger doses bought damage instead of class.

Soft prefixes (65k trained parameters on the input) and affine concept editing are likewise closed, the latter for zero GPU hours by a pre-registered offline check. The newest idea — guided decoding, recomputing guidance at every step rather than injecting one static vector — did steer its own objective decisively (+5.71, 39/40 seeds, p<10-10, compounding across chunks), but whether that produced better clusters is unanswerable from the run: 35 of 40 seeds gave identical outcomes across all arms, leaving an effective sample of five. It did yield the most useful reframing so far — when the model produces a detectable cluster it is the requested class ~92% of the time, against ~69% for a length-matched real core. Detection, not class-correctness, is the bottleneck. Three separate measurement audits this month also withdrew the PKS/NRPS-hybrid results (ceiling 0.00 at the tested length) and re-based the PKS numbers.

The through-line: every mechanism tried injects the condition at one place; the model conditions perfectly well on content present at every position, which is exactly what seeding provides. Depth was varied — the nine-layer stack, with a distinct direction and dose at each layer, returned 0/12. What the literature's per-layer modules add is not depth but training: a small gated, zero-initialised module learned end-to-end, against our hand-computed edit applied to a frozen model. Next: fix the continuation-scoped readout, then per-layer conditional adapters and per-class adapter routing. Meanwhile exemplar conditioning is a real, controlled, publishable capability — "extend and diversify a known cluster", not "generate class X de novo".

01

How we evaluate a generated sequence

Generation is scored by a pipeline of checks (independent biological measurements) that combine into questions (the pass/fail gates we actually care about). The gene caller is pyrodigal (Prodigal) throughout; antiSMASH 8 is the gold-standard arbiter of whether something is a BGC and of which class. Implementation: src/bgc_pipeline/evaluation.py; a cheap subset runs per-checkpoint (quick_eval.sh), the full suite offline (run_eval.sh).

Checks — what each one measures

antismash gold standard
Runs antiSMASH on the generated DNA: does it detect a BGC region, and is the predicted product the requested class? Recalibrated from ~0.15 → ~0.97 on real held-out cores by completing the product→class map.
coding_sanity
Is this plausible coding DNA? pyrodigal ORF calling + coding density, guarded by a dinucleotide-entropy check that rejects degenerate GC-repeat collapse (which fools a naive "≥1 complete gene" rule).
class_markers fast proxy
Pfam (pyhmmer) scan for the obligate class-defining domains — e.g. NRPS adenylation (PF00501) & condensation (PF00668); PKS KS/AT/ACP. Used when antiSMASH is skipped; ~0.87 on real cores.
module_architecture
Counts complete, ordered biosynthetic modules — NRPS C-A-T, PKS KS-AT-ACP — the defining machinery of an assembly-line cluster.
class_probe continuous · never a gate
A calibrated continuous class score, added Aug 2026 because the pass/fail checks were too coarse to detect partial effects. Valid only in paired comparisons: it has no negative class, cannot abstain, and scores artificially edited off-manifold sequences higher than genuine class members. Three tests prevent it from ever gating.
taxon_faithfulness removed Aug 2026
Graded composition against the conditioned taxon. Retired — it returned no verdict on 870/870 records, and it measures taxon conditioning rather than class.
kmer_novelty
Is the sequence novel vs the training corpus (not memorized)? Skipped in quick-eval; run in the full suite.
protein_homology
MMseqs2 homology of predicted proteins to known enzymes. DB not yet wired — diagnostic only.
protein_foldability
ESMFold pLDDT of predicted proteins. Optional / GPU-expensive; opt-in.

Questions — the gates derived from the checks

GATES  is_bgc (antiSMASH detected a cluster) · correct_class (its class matches the request) · novel (not memorized).   DIAGNOSTICS  proteins_plausible · complete (has ordered modules) · class_probe_agrees. Retired along the way: synthesis feasibility, Evo2 perplexity, BiG-SCAPE, E. coli-expressibility gating, and conditioning_faithful.

Controls are mandatory, not optional. Every reported rate carries a measured ceiling (real cores at the generations' own length and class mix) and a measured floor (real non-BGC windows — the generator refuses to substitute shuffled sequence). Measured false-positive rate for antiSMASH is_bgc: 0.000; for the retired any-Pfam proxy it was 0.960, which is why it was retired.

Read

03

Diagnosis: narrowing the suspects

A structured multi-agent read of the codebase + data established two firm negatives before any retraining:

  • Not the training data. 24/24 sampled NRPS/PKS/hybrid cores do contain their obligate domains and multi-module architecture, positioned within the training window. The signal the model fails to produce is present in what it was trained on.
  • Not a conditioning bug. The class/taxon prefix is byte-identical between training and generation — the tag reaches the model exactly as trained.

That left four testable mechanisms, which motivated the probe sweep: LoRA capacity (the long-range mixing pathway is largely frozen), chunking (long assembly-line genes are fragmented across training windows), class imbalance (simple classes as easy attractors), and a generation-window confound.

04

The probe sweep

Method. Each probe is a fast capability screen: ~350 fresh-from-base LoRA steps at L=16,384, each changing exactly one variable versus a shared control, then scored by quick-eval (n=6/probe). They are deliberately under-trained, so they're read on the sensitive markers (class_markers, obligate_fraction) plus a paired control — a change relative to control is the signal, not the absolute value.

Verdict — corrected at n=15

The n=6 screens flagged C (whole megasynthase cores) as a breakthrough — correct_class 0.33. At n=15 that does not hold: correct_class is 0.067 (1/15) for every configuration — all-classes, mega-only, and whole-core alike. No probe lifts the functional gate.

What survives is a real but sub-functional gradient. On domain-level markers the ordering whole-core > mega-only > all-classes holds up: whole megasynthase cores yield ~3–5× more class-appropriate obligate domains and partial modules (class_markers 0.33 vs 0.07; modules 0.27 vs 0.07). So whole-core + concentration push the model toward the right parts — but not yet the assembled, validated whole. Model capacity, class imbalance, chunk-labeling, and gene-aware chunking are all ruled out.

The methodological lesson that shaped this: the same C comparison read correct_class 0.33 at n=6 and 0.067 at n=15. These fast 350-step screens resolve directional effects on sensitive markers, not final gate rates — the gate question needs a real training run.

05

Other findings worth raising

Small-n evaluation noise

A single n=6 quick-eval read is_bgc = 0 at step 1,200; pooling to n=21 gave ~0.14. Headline gates need n ≥ 15 pooled before we treat a "0" as real — this changed how we read every subsequent probe.

Why probe B was worth running even though it failed

Evo2 is a hybrid model: only 5 of 32 blocks are attention; the rest are long-convolution "Hyena" blocks that do the long-range mixing. Our LoRA spends ~81% of its capacity on position-wise MLPs that cannot mix across positions, and the Hyena long-range pathway (its input projection + convolution kernels) is essentially frozen. That made "insufficient capacity" the leading structural hypothesis — but unfreezing it (B) changed nothing, cleanly ruling capacity out at this scale. Detail written up in docs/evo2_lora_and_hyena.md.

No cheap fast-iteration model

The 1B Evo2 requires Transformer Engine / FP8 (not installed) and has no bf16 fallback; only the 7B does. So there is no small model to speed up probing — everything runs on the 7B.

antiSMASH-DB version drift

The GBK archive re-parsed for per-gene coordinates is a different antiSMASH-DB version than the one that originally built splits_core — region boundaries diverge for ~45% of records. Caught during a validation step; resolved by building the gene-aware probe dataset self-consistently from the re-parse. Flagged as a provenance item to reconcile before the production rebuild.

Why gene-aware still didn't help — an informative null

We first confirmed gene-aware chunking does what it claims: blind chunking never shows 36% of the large (≥3 kb) megasynthase-scale genes complete; gene-aware halves that to 16%, rescuing ~11,400 big genes across 48.8% of cores. Yet generation did not improve. The lesson: keeping individual genes whole isn't enough — a long cluster is still fragmented across windows, so the model never sees the whole assembly line. That redirects attention to whole-cluster context (window length) and to the residual floor of 6,497 genes individually larger than the 16 kb window, which no boundary scheme can keep whole.

06

Implementation changes going into this direction

Training

  • --gene-aware-chunking + build_gene_aware_chunk_spans — packs complete genes into each window and snaps cuts to gene gaps, with an arithmetic fallback for single genes larger than the window. Unit-tested (no cut lands inside a gene) and a window-count-explosion bug fixed.
  • --lora-target-parameters — adapts the frozen Hyena projection weights via PEFT target_parameters (the mechanism behind probe B).
  • EVO2_BASE_MODEL env override — swap the base checkpoint without code changes (defaults to the 7B).

Data

  • build_core_records.py now persists strict_core_gene_bounds (per-gene coordinates, previously parsed then discarded) and takes a --genomes-allowlist for fast targeted re-parsing.
  • build_probe_subsets.py — whole-core, mega-upweighted, and long-mega gene-aware datasets.

Evaluation

  • quick_eval.sh gained TEMPERATURE / TOP_K / TOP_P and MAX_WINDOWS / CHUNK_OVERLAP env pass-throughs — for decoding-temperature and chained-generation sweeps (both used to rule out the sampling and window confounds).
07

The real-scale test — and the decision it forces

We took the leading fix to a real run: a milestone-gated, mega-only whole-core fine-tune (L=32,768, fresh-from-base), evaluated at n=15 every ~2 epochs and set to auto-stop if the gate stayed at the floor. It stopped itself.

Milestone (n=15)is_bgccorrect_classclass_markersmodules
~epoch 2 (step 120)0.270.1330.200.20

More training made it worse. A flicker at epoch 2 (correct_class 2/15, 3/15 modules) collapsed to zero by epoch 4, and the run auto-killed. Whole-core training at feasible single-GPU context does not convert to correct-class generation — and the decline points at overfitting the small whole-core set (80 Mbp; keeping cores whole at L=32k drops 62% of the megasynthase nucleotides — the long, multi-module cores).

So every single-GPU-cheap lever is now exhausted — capacity, imbalance, chunk labeling, gene-aware chunking, and whole-core-at-scale. What remains is structural:

  • Long context (multi-GPU). Train whole clusters without dropping the long ones — the data-starvation this run exposed. L ≥ 131k needs 2–4× H100 + sequence parallelism (~1–2 weeks + cost). The honest test of the whole-cluster hypothesis.
  • Higher LoRA rank — tested, negative. The r=16/64/128 sweep is in: correct_class stayed at the floor (0.067 / 0.067 / 0.0), and r=128 was worse. r=64 did nudge domain markers (class_markers 0.13→0.27), but — like every lever — it didn't reach the gate. With coverage-probe B, that closes the capacity question entirely: neither which layers we adapt nor how expressively is the limiter.
  • Full / partial fine-tuning instead of LoRA — more capacity, more compute.
  • Reposition Evo2 as a scorer / evaluator of BGCs rather than a de-novo generator of these hard classes — where the recalibrated antiSMASH / eval stack is already strong.

The bottom line for the talk: the failure is well-characterized and localized to the training signal, and every cheap fix — including a real-scale whole-core run — has now been tested and does not lift the functional gate. The pattern (the real run degrading with training) suggests we are near the ceiling of LoRA-conditioned Evo2 for de-novo megasynthase generation. The question in front of us is which structural lever — long-context, higher-capacity fine-tuning, or repositioning the model as an evaluator — is worth committing to. A genuine fork, worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting into.

08

Why class conditioning is hard

Two things followed the whole-core negative. We asked a question we had never actually tested — does conditioning work for the simple classes? — and we established, at the mechanism level, why class conditioning may be fundamentally hard for this model.

The failure is general, not just megasynthases

Every prior evaluation only ever requested NRPS / PKS / hybrid. So we asked the all-classes models for the easy targets — terpene (often a single cyclase), ectoine (a 3-gene cluster) — where sequence length is no obstacle, at n=15/class alongside the previously-missing base-Evo2 (no-adapter) control:

Simple classes · n=15/class (75 total)is_bgccorrect_classcoding density
v2 adapter0.120.0130.893
base Evo2 (no adapter)0.000.000.606

The single correct hit across 75 v2 generations was one ectoine; the preview's apparent terpene flicker was small-n noise. So it is not assembly-line complexity that defeats the model — the conditioning interface is weak across the board. The base control is the informative part: the LoRA clearly does contribute — it lifts coding density 0.61 → 0.89 and is_bgc 0 → 0.12 — but adds nothing to class (both ≈0 correct_class). The adapter learned to write BGC-like DNA, not the requested class.

The mechanism: Evo2 has no native notion of "class"

A codebase + literature + adversarial investigation (all converging, with citations) established what the base model was actually trained to condition on. The answer: only phylogeny. Evo2's single structured conditioning field is the GTDB lineage tag (|d__Bacteria;p__…;s__…|), plus two structural contig-stitch tokens. There is no native handle for biosynthetic product class anywhere in pretraining.

Our prefix splits cleanly against that: the taxonomy half is already byte-for-byte Evo2's native format, but the |COMPOUND_CLASS:NRPS| half is something the model never saw and has no prior for. LoRA is therefore installing "class → sequence" entirely from scratch, through a low-rank bottleneck. Tellingly, Arc's own functional conditioning (cas9/cas12/cas13) was a separate fine-tuning stage using exactly this prepend-a-token-then-finetune pattern — confirming there is no base prior to tap, and that installing functional control takes a real finetune (a tall order for a multi-gene assembly line).

Reframe

The ceiling isn't only LoRA capacity — the base model offers no product-class prior to amplify, so the adapter must build the concept from nothing. This retires "align the prefix to the native format" as a class lever: the taxonomy is already aligned, and class has no native form to align to.

The amplification test — and its answer

Classifier-free guidance (CFG). At each generation step, run the model twice — once with the class tag, once without — and push the output along the difference by a factor w: logits = uncond + w·(cond − uncond). At w=1 this is ordinary generation; w>1 exaggerates whatever the class tag was doing. It needs no retraining — it reuses the existing adapter and changes only how tokens are sampled. A built-in gate (w=1 must reproduce an independent from-scratch decode) confirmed the implementation before we trusted the rest.

Amplifying the class direction did not raise correct_class — it destroyed coherence. As w climbs, correct_class goes 0.067 → 0 → 0 while the DNA degrades toward gibberish (coding density 0.90 → 0.26 → 0.00), with no transient bump — the signature of no class signal to amplify. Honest caveat: v2 was never trained with class-dropout, so the "unconditional" stream is a proxy rather than a learned null, and the high-w collapse is partly the expected out-of-distribution failure. It does not fully rule out that a properly trained null would behave better — but there is no positive signal here, and it is consistent with the mechanism finding.

Where this left the fork

Every prefix-conditioning lever came back negative: conditioning fails generally, the adapter adds coherence but not class, and CFG found nothing to amplify (a finer sweep confirmed correct_class is flat through the coherent regime, then collapses with it). That closed the label-conditioning question — and sent us to the two decisive follow-ups below.

09

The decisive pair: the model knows the class — it just won't take a label

Two experiments settled what all the negatives meant. The first asks whether Evo2 represents compound class at all; the second asks whether the one thing that did work — seeding with a real gene fragment — survives scrutiny.

Evo2 already encodes class — with no fine-tuning

We fed raw core DNA with no prefix and no class tag (otherwise a probe could simply read the tag), captured mid-network activations, and fit a linear classifier for compound class against a shuffled-label control.

Linear probe (n=991, 11 classes)balanced accuracychanceshuffled control
base Evo2 (no fine-tuning)0.9110.0910.089
v2 adapter0.9060.0910.082

Class is linearly decodable at ten times chance from the untouched base model. Every class clears chance comfortably — even the hard megasynthases (NRPS 0.80, PKS 0.86, hybrid 0.73), with the simple ones at 0.93–0.99. Two consequences follow. First, the adapter added nothing to the representation (0.906 vs 0.911) — the knowledge was always there. Second, the depth profile is the mechanism: accuracy climbs to 0.911 at layer 16, then decays to 0.41 by the final layer. The class is encoded mid-network and is not carried into the next-token distribution.

The reframe

This is a steering problem, not a knowledge problem. Every previous attempt tried to teach the model a class concept it already had. It also hands us a tool: a layer-16 classifier at 0.91 accuracy is a single matrix multiply — cheap enough to run inside the generation loop as a class scorer.

The seeding result survives — but the class comes from the exemplar

A seven-arm controlled experiment (n=15/class) stress-tested the earlier seeding lift against every artifact we could think of, with the seed sequence held identical across arms.

The effect is real: every generation passed the memorization check (nearest-training-cluster containment maxed at 0.024, so nothing is copied); it survives truncating the seed so that no gene spans into the continuation (0.217 — the class-defining machinery appears de novo); and it collapses to zero on a shuffled seed, so it is the real gene content doing the work, not composition. But the last row reinterprets it. When the tag names a different class than the seed, the output follows the seed (0.317), not the tag (0.067) — and dropping the tag entirely changes nothing (0.283 either way). The tag is inert; the exemplar carries the class. So the honest description is exemplar-conditioned generation — "extend and diversify this cluster" — not label-conditioned de-novo design. The effect is ~0.28, below the 0.37 the small pilot suggested.

Where the project stands

Evo2 knows the class, won't take a label, and will take an exemplar. That is a coherent and genuinely publishable result: a strong negative (abstract-label conditioning is unreachable through a low-rank adapter — and we now know mechanistically why: the class signal fades before the output layer) paired with a verified positive (real, novel, correct-class clusters from an exemplar). The next phase follows directly and needs no retraining: activation steering — inject the probe's class direction into layer-16 activations during generation — and guided decoding, using that same probe as an in-loop scorer, composed with exemplar seeding. Notably this de-prioritizes both per-class adapters and the multi-GPU long-context run, since each aimed to install a class representation the model turns out to already have.

10

Activation steering: the full programme, and why it is closed

If the model encodes class but ignores the label, the obvious move is to write the class in directly — add the probe's class direction to the residual stream during generation. We built the machinery, fixed a serious instrument bug in it, and ran it to exhaustion. It does not work, and we now know precisely why.

The programme

PhaseWhat it testedResult
0–1 — rebuildDirections rebuilt after the length-axis bug, length-stripped, held-out scored, 10 shuffled-label controls per layervalid all 11 classes beat null at all 4 layers
3 — does it control class?Paired arms at doses 1 / 2 / 4 class-units, real vs shuffled-label directionnegative 0/48, 2/46, 0/46 target markers

Phase 5 falsified dilution in reverse. The intuition was that an edit made early gets washed out, so editing nearer the output should hit harder. The opposite is true: the residual stream is additive, so an edit at layer 16 is carried forward and re-read by 11 more blocks, while an edit at layer 27 has only four. "Closer to the output" means fewer opportunities to be used.

Phase 6 added a continuous readout, and it is what made the null legible. The effect on the target class is null at every dose — the direction installs nothing. Its companion signal, that the direction reliably removes a class present in the seed, did not survive refitting the probe train-only and is withdrawn; only Phase 1's teacher-forced ablation asymmetry stands. Every binary gate reported all of this as a flat zero.

The closing argument — August 11

Every null above was compatible with two very different stories, and we had never separated them: the edit never landed (in which case our dosing was wrong and steering deserved another try), or it landed and was ignored (in which case steering-at-a-layer is structurally dead). We had assumed the second and written "stop trying steering variants" into the plan on that assumption. A GPU-free audit settled it, and was written so it could overturn our own conclusion.

Verdict — the edit landed, and was ignored

At every depth we ever steered, the edit was 1.5–10× larger than needed to completely convert a linear readout in the very layer it was applied to — and the output still did not follow. The failure is downstream of the edit. Steering-at-a-layer is closed for a measured reason rather than an assumed one.

The delete/install asymmetry is not geometric. Ablation also works in activation space (class probability 0.80 → 0.09–0.41 at every layer). Both operations succeed there; only deletion survives to the output. The asymmetry is about how the model reads that space, which kills "the install direction is malformed" as an explanation.

And we had been overdosing, badly

A follow-up placed each edit relative to where the target class actually lives. A dose of 2.8 class-units lands 3.4 standard deviations past the target class mean; 11.4 lands 19.5 sd past it, and twelve times further from the real class than genuine members sit from each other. We were not nudging sequences toward a class — we were pushing them off the data distribution entirely. That is the mechanism behind the long-standing observation that a bigger dose buys damage, not class.

This does not reopen steering: Phase 3 already ran dose 1 — the correctly-calibrated, on-target dose — and got 0/48.

11

Soft prefixes, and a cheap negative that closed the next idea too

If a written label is inert, perhaps a learned one is not. We trained a per-class continuous prefix — 16 vectors × 4,096 dimensions, 65k free parameters per class — prepended to the input and optimized directly on next-token loss.

It trained cleanly and each class separated from an identical initialization, so the optimization was real. It bought ~0.003 nats and produced correct_class 0/12 generated de novo. The bound is narrow and worth stating precisely: 65k parameters changing only the input. It does not bound a per-class adapter or a genuinely trainable class token.

Affine Concept Editing — closed for zero GPU hours

The next ranked idea was ACE: instead of adding a class direction, ablate the current class and reset the coordinate to the real class mean. Writing it out showed it is the same rank-1 edit along the same direction, differing only in using a per-example dose — arithmetically, "dose 1.0, applied per sequence."

So the deciding question was not whether it moves the probe, but whether the edited activation resembles a real member of the class. It does not: ACE removes only 6.7% (layer 16) and 11.6% (layer 27) of the distance to the real class manifold — negative for two classes. A rank-1 edit corrects one coordinate out of 4,096 and leaves the other 4,095 belonging to the source class. Closed on a pre-registered rule, before any GPU time was spent.

What our instruments can and cannot see

Three separate audits this month found that measurement, not modelling, was limiting what we could conclude. These change how earlier numbers should be read.

Sequence length: two classes were never testable at 3 kb

Nearly every generation experiment ran at 2–3 kb, while real cores are often far longer. We truncated real held-out cores to the lengths we generate and re-ran the same gate — the best case any generated sequence could achieve.

PKS results at 3 kb must be quoted against 0.40, not 1.0. The other three classes are only mildly affected, so this does not invalidate the programme as a whole.

Statistical power, stated honestly

Several experiments used n=12 arms. Against a ~2% base rate, that design can only detect a jump to ≈23%. Those "0/12" results mean no large effect, not no effect. The pooled Phase 3 comparison (n=140) resolves ≈6.5%. Direction estimation was never the problem — split-half agreement is 0.97–0.99 at n=500/class.

The class probe is a comparator, never a gate

We added a continuous class score precisely because the binary gates were too coarse to see Phase 6. It is calibrated at both ends and it is never allowed to decide anything on its own — three tests enforce this. The ACE audit supplied the mechanism for that rule: in 10 of 10 class-layer cells, an artificially edited off-manifold activation scored higher than a genuine member of the class (real NRPS 0.762 vs an edit at 0.963 sitting 3.9× further from the real NRPS manifold). A linear readout of one direction out of 4,096 becomes more confident as the point becomes less like the real thing.

13

Guided decoding: selection worked, the ruler could not see it

The first idea from a structured literature sweep, and structurally different from everything above: rather than injecting one static vector, recompute guidance at every step from what has been generated so far. Generate four candidate continuations per 600-nt chunk, keep the one a discriminator scores highest for the target class, repeat. Two pre-registered questions, deliberately separated.

Q1 — did selection move what we selected on?

Yes, decisively. Guide score, guided vs random control: +5.71 pooled, 39 of 40 seeds, p<10-10, with the advantage growing across chunks. A stricter test — comparing the guided arm's pick against the control's own best available candidate — confirms it: chunk 0 gives 40/40 exact ties (both arms provably share candidate sets, a free harness control), then a gap opening to +0.395 by chunk 4 (31/34, p=10-6). Selection is real and it compounds.

Underpowered, and the ruler was confounded — not a null

35 of 40 seeds returned an identical outcome in all three arms, leaving an effective sample of five informative seeds. With zero reversals anywhere in 120 records, the sign test needs 6–0 to reach p<0.05; we observed 5–0. The design missed significance by one seed.

A same-day retraction, and the reframing it produced

This result was first reported with a second conclusion attached: that correct_class agreeing with is_bgc on 117 of 120 records meant the metric had collapsed into mere detection, because antiSMASH was recognising the real seed inside the scored sequence. That was wrong. The seed is not in the scored sequence and never has been — both generators score the continuation only, and across every seeded run ever produced, 0 of 1,512 stored sequences begin with their seed. antiSMASH has always been reading purely generated DNA. Nothing needs rerunning, and the 0.283 exemplar result is cleaner than the retraction implied.

The error came from reading a concordance rate without ever measuring the same rate on a control. On real held-out cores truncated to the lengths we generate, antiSMASH's off-class rate is substantial:

The reframing — arguably the most useful thing to come out of this run

Nearly a third of antiSMASH detections on real DNA at 3 kb are the wrong class, so the "detected but off-class" outcome is common and the metric discriminates perfectly well. The 97.5% concordance is therefore a fact about our generations, not about the ruler.

When the model produces a detectable cluster, it is the requested class about 92% of the time — against roughly 69% for a length-matched real core. The failure mode is not "builds clusters of the wrong class". It is "usually builds nothing recognisable, but lands on-class when it does." Detection is the bottleneck, not class-correctness — which points effort at making anything recognisable at all, rather than at steering class.

Recorded as a methodological rule: the retraction survived a five-reviewer adversarial pass because the confound was handed to reviewers as background context rather than as a claim to attack. A premise supplied to a verifier is not verified by it.

This result was adjudicated by five independent adversarial reviewers before being recorded, which overturned our first reading of it. The initial interpretation — that the model had learned to game the guide — is wrong: the guide predicts the independent readout at AUC 0.856 on sequences it played no part in selecting. It is a good proxy, not a gamed one. What is real is a narrower effect: selection degrades the guide's calibration, with the score on antiSMASH-negatives inflating from 0.141 to 0.613.

14

Where this leaves the project

The one thing that does work

Exemplar conditioning is a validated capability. Seed the model with a real fragment of a cluster and it extends it in the right class — correct_class 0.283 against a 0.067 mismatched floor, with memorization ruled out and all four pre-registered controls passed. The class comes from the seed, never from the label. Framed honestly, that is "extend and diversify a known cluster", not "generate class X de novo" — and it is a publishable capability today. A same-day scare that this number was confounded by the seed was retracted: the scored sequence contains no seed, verified across all 1,512 seeded records ever produced.

What is definitively closed

  • Written class labels — inert, in both the LoRA and base model.
  • Classifier-free guidance — no amplifiable class signal.
  • Activation steering, in every variant of layer, dose and direction recipe. The edit lands and is ignored; a further variant is not worth running.
  • Soft prefixes at 65k parameters on the input.
  • Affine concept editing — a rank-1 edit cannot reach the class manifold.
  • Model capacity — LoRA rank, target modules, and unfreezing the long-range pathway all ruled out earlier.

The last inference-time door, closed by a positive result

Every closure above is a null: we tried something and nothing happened. The final experiment is different, and it is the one that makes the conclusion safe. Activation patching substitutes a genuine internal state — recorded from a real forward pass over real DNA of another class, in-distribution, every one of its 4,096 dimensions mutually consistent — rather than the hand-built direction steering used.

So the model reads its mid-layer state perfectly well. Substituting 1% of the context moves its behaviour 41% of the way to the donor; 20% of the context moves it 92%. Our steering edit — applied to every position, at up to 11.4 class-units — moved it essentially nothing. Every steering null was about the shape of the edit, not the model's blindness, exactly as the ACE geometry predicted.

And yet class still does not travel — 0 of 48

Running the same transplant and then generating 3 kb, scored by antiSMASH: the donor's compound class appears zero times in 48 transplants (95% upper bound 6.1%), against an unpatched recipient-class rate of 0.333. If class travelled as well as behaviour does at these settings, it should have appeared roughly 16 times. The degradation that does occur is generic — a same-class transplant disrupts identically — so there is not even the delete-without-install asymmetry steering showed.

This is the strongest closure in the project, because it rests on a positive demonstration rather than a null. The channel provably works. The model provably reads it. Its local output provably follows. And the multi-kilobase organisation that constitutes a compound class does not. Class is not controllable from a mid-layer representation at generation time — which leaves training-time coupling as the only route.

The reframing — class conditioning was the smaller problem

Two measurements on 12 August, both on data already on disk, changed what this project should be working on. The first decomposes the headline metric into its two factors:

regimenP(detected)95% CIP(right class | detected)
de novo (no seed)810.012[0.000, 0.067]1 detection — unestimable
seeded1200.367[0.281, 0.459]0.932

The seed multiplies detection 30×. In the seeded regime class-correctness is already 0.932 — roughly 7% is available to any conditioning mechanism. De novo, detection is 0.012, so a mechanism that installed class perfectly would have nothing to install it into. In neither regime is class-correctness the binding constraint, which means the entire conditioning programme — including the per-layer adapters that were next on the list — was aimed at the smaller of two problems.

Why the label was always inert, in the units the model is trained on

The second measurement scores the same 500 bases while varying only how much preceding DNA the model may see.

context given10 nt1003001,0006,000

A blind guess over the four bases is 1.386 nats. Ten bases of context already delivers 73% of everything the model achieves; going from 1,000 to 6,000 buys 0.005. All long-range context is worth 0.149 nats — and against that budget, the right class tag versus the wrong one is worth −0.0006 nats (−0.0000 with the tag only 200 bases away).

One number explains three years of nulls

The tag is not ignored out of stubbornness — using it never reduced the loss. Gradient descent builds what pays; a pathway carrying "NRPS" across 30,000 positions pays nothing, while "look harder at the last 60 bases" pays everywhere. The inert label, the absent CFG signal and the 0.003-nat soft prefix are one phenomenon, not three.

What is actually broken: the model cannot hold a reading frame

antiSMASH needs clustered genes, so before concluding a capability failure we re-scored with far more permissive instruments — does the DNA code for proteins, how long are the reading frames, and does any single class-defining domain appear anywhere, no clustering required.

Two instruments at very different strictness agree, so this is capability rather than measurement. And the diagnosis is specific: de novo output is not junk — but its longest reading frame is 332–505 amino acids against ~1,000–1,500 for a single NRPS module. The model runs out of frame before it can encode one module, so there is nowhere for a catalytic domain to sit.

A ladder to replace a gate that has read zero de novo since the project began

This finally supplies intermediate targets that are non-zero today and can be optimised and tracked: max_orf_aa → domain_count → antiSMASH detection → class. You cannot optimise against a metric that is always zero; you can absolutely optimise reading-frame length.

The organising hypothesis

Every mechanism we have tried injects the class condition at one place — one input token, one input position, an activation edit at a handful of hand-picked layers. The model conditions perfectly well on content present at every position, which is exactly what seeding provides. Across nine independent search angles in the literature sweep, the recurring answer is that conditioning which works enters at every layer, through a small gated module trained end-to-end. What separates ours from the literature's per-layer modules is not depth — depth was varied at nine layers and returned 0/12 — but that theirs are trained end-to-end while ours was a fixed, hand-computed vector.

Dataset & provenance

The model trains on splits_core — a deliberately curated slice of the antiSMASH database, not the raw thing. The raw DB is ~7× larger; the reduction is what makes the evaluation trustworthy and the training tractable.

Deduplication, not arbitrary capping, drives the 456k→123k cut; the rest is class-balancing (so TERPENE/RIPP don't swamp rare classes), genome-disjoint + cross-split dedup (so val/test are genuinely unseen), strict-core trimming, and holding out MiBIG.

Why this matters for the result

The megasynthase classes were cut ~90–95% (NRPS 73,259 raw → 3,445 kept), but almost entirely by deduplication — NRPS lands below the per-class cap, so the raw regions are mostly near-duplicates and the ~3–4k we keep are roughly the genuinely distinct cores in the whole database. So the generation failure is not a data-volume problem: we already have essentially all the diverse megasynthase cores that exist, and "more data" would just mean more near-duplicates. That is consistent with the ceiling conclusion — it points at model/approach, not dataset size.
